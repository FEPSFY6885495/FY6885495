    • Abstract

    Black holes and Quasars have been the biggest what if questions in physics since their discovery over 70 years ago .The phenomena behind how these great giants even exist, has been the greatest mystery in science today. Black holes, regions in the universe that have a gravitation pull so powerful, it can bend the laws of space and time. Quasars are galactic nucleus centred around a supermassive black hole more than 10⁶ times the mass of the sun, constantly being fed enough matter to eject huge amounts of energy in the form of X-rays that can reach distances beyond 200,000 light years (CGP Books, 2018, p. 455). In this article, we will be exploring the nature and formation of these cosmic beasts, unveiling their significance in the shaping of galaxies and deepening our understanding of the cosmos.

    The Cosmic Paradox

    The universe is ever expanding and filled with many mysteries that are still yet to be discovered. Among these mysteries is the nature of the black hole, an abyss so deep and dark that it consumes everything in its path, a cosmic vacuum from which nothing can escape, not even light (CGP Books, 2018, p. 455). These cosmic predators roam the vast expanse of the universe, feeding on anything that comes in their way, from stars to entire solar systems (Throne, Price and Macdonald,1986). Although due to their nature of being so dark it is almost impossible to see them, however they leave a cosmological footprint everywhere they go, marking their presence across the universe in ways that are impossible for us to miss (Youvan,2023). Quasars on the other hand, are the very essence of light and dark, the very pinnacle of the universes extreme, powered by supermassive blackholes at the centres of galaxies. Quasars are also known as Quasi-Stellar objects, what gives them their nature and distinguishes them from supermassive black holes, is their ability to outshine their host galaxy (Vestergaard and Gültekin, 2024). This occurs when a supermassive black hole is actively pulling in matter forming an accretion disk, due to the intense level of heat, friction and sheer force of gravity, enormous amounts of energy across the electromagnetic spectrum is emitted (Barrios,2024). Quasars are among the brightest objects in the universe, providing a window into the past for scientists to study, offering a glimpse into the early stages of the universe and the formation of galaxies.

    Discovery And Birth Of Black Holes And Einsteins Theory Of General Relativity

    The black hole was first theorised in the late 1916s by the famous physicist Karl Schwarzschild, following Einsteins theory of general relativity. He discovered that objects of sufficient mass would have an escape velocity greater than the speed of light and, therefore would not be observable (Britannica, 2024). Black holes vary in size. Stellar black holes are formed when the core of a star approximately 20 times or more the mass of the sun collapses in on itself (NASA, 2024), the neutrons within the star can no longer withstand the force of gravity causing it to collapse and implode to an infinitely dense point. At this point the laws of physics are no longer obeyed. A black hole is born, an object that will consume everything and anything in its path. The theory of general relativity is that spacetime was thought to be like a sheet of stretched fabric, placing an object of heavy mass such as a star or planet essentially would cause a curvature in spacetime, where light would bend around these massive objects, this is also known as gravitational lensing (Tillman, Bartels and Dutfield, 2024). For a black hole however, it brings these natures to the extremes. The curvature caused by a black hole is infinitely steep, bending everything even light towards a point of singularity. This is the point where physics as we know breaks down.

    Beyond The Event Horizon

    The event horizon, the point of no return, this is the point on a black hole where its escape velocity is equal to the speed of light, anything past this point and it cannot escape, not even light. A black hole’s key feature is its ability to warp space time by creating a dent in the space time “fabric” so large due to its mass and gravity that it distorts space and time, creating regions of large curvature. Due to this warping of space-time, there are many effects such as gravitational lensing and time dilation, this is where, as you approach the event horizon time slows down if not effectively stops relative to an observer watching from afar. Another key feature of a black hole is that as matter falls inside it can trigger the emission of high energy x rays in the form of jets reaching lengths of thousands of kilometres (CGP Books, 2018, p. 455).

    Ancient Gateways Into The Past

    Quasars were first discovered in the 1950s and were presumed to be stars. However, their spectra were nothing like of stars and sometimes even shot out jets of material. Later it was revealed that these so called “stars” were not really stars and were found to be the extraordinarily bright cores of distant galaxies that were enormously red shifted. This huge red shift suggested that these galaxies were over distances of billion of light years away (CGP Books, 2018, p. 454). Quasars are powered by gas and matter which spiral into a supermassive black hole at unimaginable velocity, forming an accretion disk which emits matter and radiation in the form of jets. Quasars allows us to understand the conditions of the early universe. Their enormous red shift tells astronomers approximately how far away they are, the most distant quasar being over 13 billion light years away (Gohd, 2021), this means that light began its journey to us shortly after the Big Bang. These ancient gateways of the past, provides a window for astronomers to study the early stages of the cosmos, the formation of galaxies and the growth of supermassive black holes.
